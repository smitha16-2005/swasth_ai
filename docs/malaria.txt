MALARIA - CLINICAL GUIDELINES (ICMR/NVBDCP)

DEFINITION:
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites
transmitted through infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

SYMPTOMS:
- Cyclical fever with chills (every 48-72 hours)
- Sweating episodes following fever
- Severe headache
- Muscle pain and fatigue
- Nausea and vomiting
- Anaemia

WARNING SIGNS (Severe Malaria - Immediate Hospital):
- Impaired consciousness or seizures
- Severe anaemia
- Respiratory distress
- Abnormal bleeding
- Jaundice
- Haemoglobinuria (dark urine)

RISK CLASSIFICATION:
RED - seizures, unconsciousness, severe anaemia, respiratory distress
YELLOW - cyclical fever with chills, recent travel to forest/rural area
GREEN - mild fever without cyclical pattern, no travel history

TREATMENT PROTOCOL:
Plasmodium vivax: Chloroquine + Primaquine (14 days)
Plasmodium falciparum: Artemisinin Combination Therapy (ACT)
Severe malaria: IV Artesunate - hospital only

RISK FACTORS:
- Travel to forested or tribal areas
- Living near stagnant water
- No mosquito net usage
- Previous malaria history

WHEN TO REFER:
Any suspected severe malaria should be referred immediately.
Rapid Diagnostic Test (RDT) should be performed at PHC level.
