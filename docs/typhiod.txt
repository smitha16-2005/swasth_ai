TYPHOID FEVER - CLINICAL GUIDELINES (ICMR)

DEFINITION:
Typhoid is a bacterial infection caused by Salmonella Typhi,
spread through contaminated food and water.

SYMPTOMS:
- Sustained fever that increases gradually each day
- Headache
- Weakness and fatigue
- Stomach pain
- Constipation or diarrhoea
- Rose-coloured spots on chest (rare)
- Loss of appetite

WARNING SIGNS (Severe Typhoid - Immediate Hospital):
- Intestinal perforation (sudden severe abdominal pain)
- Intestinal bleeding
- Encephalopathy (confusion, altered consciousness)
- Myocarditis

RISK CLASSIFICATION:
RED - severe abdominal pain, bleeding, confusion
YELLOW - fever worsening daily for more than 5 days, weakness
GREEN - mild fever less than 3 days, no warning signs

TREATMENT PROTOCOL:
First line: Azithromycin or Cefixime (outpatient)
Severe cases: IV Ceftriaxone (hospital)
Duration: 7-14 days

PREVENTION:
- Safe drinking water
- Proper sanitation
- Typhoid vaccination
- Hand hygiene

WHEN TO REFER:
Refer immediately if intestinal complications suspected.
