TUBERCULOSIS - CLINICAL GUIDELINES (NTEP/ICMR)

DEFINITION:
TB is a bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis,
primarily affecting the lungs.

SYMPTOMS:
- Cough lasting more than 2 weeks
- Blood in sputum
- Chest pain
- Fever (low grade, usually evening)
- Night sweats
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue and weakness

WARNING SIGNS (Severe TB - Immediate Hospital):
- Coughing large amounts of blood
- Severe difficulty breathing
- High fever with rapid deterioration
- Meningitis symptoms (stiff neck, headache)

RISK CLASSIFICATION:
RED - massive haemoptysis, respiratory failure, TB meningitis
YELLOW - cough more than 2 weeks with any of the above symptoms
GREEN - brief cough without other symptoms, low risk

TREATMENT PROTOCOL:
NTEP standard regimen:
Intensive phase (2 months): HRZE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol)
Continuation phase (4 months): HR (Isoniazid, Rifampicin)
Drug Resistant TB: Refer to DRTB centre

CONTACT TRACING:
All household contacts must be screened.
Children under 5 in contact should receive preventive therapy.

WHEN TO REFER:
All presumptive TB cases should be tested with CBNAAT/TrueNat.
Drug resistant cases must be referred to DRTB centre.
